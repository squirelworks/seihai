# The built-in tutorial

Seihai ships a short tutorial that loads from its own built-in assets: five
levels, a tile sheet and tile tables of its own, all original and generated by
`tools/gen_tutorial.py` (called from `tools/gen_default_assets.py`, so
`--check` covers it). Dated 2026-10-31.

## How to get there

- **No assets folder:** "Play offline" (or a plain offline run with no
  `--level`) starts in `seihai_tutorial1.nw`. Before the tutorial this was
  `new1.nw`, which is still built in.
- **With an assets folder:** "Play the tutorial" in the start window, or
  `--level seihai_tutorial1.nw`. The levels are found in the built-in set
  because no Graal folder has files of these names.
- **Online:** never. Built-in levels are offline only (`default-assets.md`).

## The levels

| file | lesson |
|--|--|
| `seihai_tutorial1.nw` | The meadow. Walking, reading signs (walk up into them), the cottage door (a link), the path east. Start: (30, 30), on the path below the welcome sign. |
| `seihai_tutorial_house.nw` | The cottage's room. A note on the wall (a sign record on wall tiles), a chest with `glove1` (A while facing it), the door mat back out. |
| `seihai_tutorial2.nw` | The garden. A hedge of bushes closes the yard: cut them (S) or lift (A) and throw (A or S) them. Tall grass to cut, pots to throw. A wall of black stones with one grey stone in the path: it needs the glove. |
| `seihai_tutorial3.nw` | The pond. Deep water to swim, an island with a chest in the lake, a bridge, and a shallow ford. |
| `seihai_tutorial4.nw` | The rock yard. A terrace edge to jump down (stairs lead back up), a block with arrows to push, a block with a ring to pull, and a glowing ring that takes the player back to the meadow. |

Each level links to the next and back; the edges that are not links are
trees. `crates/seihai-mae/tests/tutorial_levels.rs` checks that every link
lands on open ground outside the links of its level, that every lesson's
target can be walked or swum to, that the hedge, the grey stone and the two
blocks really close their ways until the player uses them, and that cutting,
lifting with and without the glove, swimming, the ledge jump, the push and
the pull work on these levels with these tables.

## Its own sheet and tables

The tutorial must look the same whether or not the player has set a Graal
folder, so it does not use `pics1.png` from the asset layers. While the level
on screen is a tutorial level (its name starts with `seihai_tutorial`, any
case: `assets::tutorial::is_tutorial_level`):

- the view is drawn with the built-in `tutorial/pics1.png` (2048 x 512, the
  Graal sheet layout);
- the session's tile types and cut, lift, ledge and block tables are those of
  the built-in `tutorial/arrays.dat`.

`OfflineSession::load_level` swaps them in on entering such a level and puts
the player's own back on leaving it (`tutorial_tables`,
`tutorial_tables_active`). A tile table loaded while the tutorial is up goes
to the player's side and comes back on leaving. This is what a Graal client
does with tiledefs (a sheet per level by name prefix; Graal's own offline
tutorial used `picso.png,offlinetutorial,1`), done here for one built-in
prefix only, because how a client matches tiledefs prefixes is not settled
(`assets/tiledefs.rs`).

### The ids

The ids the client gives a meaning keep it, with new art: grass 0, chest
ground 2047, bush 2, stone 34, sign 512, pot 684, black stone 990, tall grass
420, what they leave (677, 679, 1802, 1770, 1834), chests 1784 / 829, deep
water 322, shallow water 398. Everything else the tutorial draws sits in ids
2560 to 4095, which only this sheet and table use:

- grass variants, and two sets of 16 corner-blended tiles (grass to path,
  grass to deep water; one bit per tile corner, so paths and shores have
  rounded edges);
- trees (4 x 4), pines (2 x 3), flower beds, fences, small rocks, reeds,
  lilies, mushrooms;
- the cottage (8 x 7, door tiles open), the terrace face (1 x 3, in the
  down-ledge list), stairs, the push and pull blocks, the bridge, the warp
  ring;
- the room: floor, back wall with windows, shelf, hearth, a note, bed, table,
  chairs, rug, plants, the door mat, the dark outside.

### Tables (`tutorial/arrays.dat`)

The same big-endian layout as a Graal `arrays.dat`, written by the generator
from the tutorial's own type list:

| array | content |
|--|--|
| 0 to 6 | passable, liquid, thrown-over, lava (empty), chairs, bed tops, bed bottoms |
| 8 | ledges by facing (up, left, down, right); only down is used |
| 9, 10 | cut: bush, tall grass; and what they leave |
| 11, 12 | lift: bush, sign, pot, stone, black stone (the client's order, so glove 1 lifts up to the pot and `glove1` adds the stone); and what they leave |
| 16 | blocks: push, pull |

The other arrays are empty or absent; the break effects and the carried
objects come from the player's `sprites.png` as before.

### Chests

Chest records are drawn over the board. The tutorial's chest art has no
ground of its own: its background is the key colour `#FF00FF`, which the
renderer leaves out for chests (`render::tiles::CHEST_KEY`), so a chest stands
on grass outside and on the floor indoors. Graal sheets have no pixel of that
colour, so their chests draw as before.

## Originality

Every pixel comes from `tools/gen_tutorial.py`: shapes, shading rules (a
sphere lit from the upper left, quantised facets for rocks), seeded noise and
a hand-picked palette. No image of any Graal client or playerworld was opened
for it. Licence: the repository's (`MIT OR Apache-2.0`), as for the rest of
the built-in set.

## Changing it

Edit `tools/gen_tutorial.py`, then `python3 tools/gen_default_assets.py`,
then `cargo test -p seihai-mae --test tutorial_levels`. A new file under
`assets/default/` also needs its line in `builtin::FILES`.
